# Phase 8 — Explainability

By the end of Phase 7, we had a working multimodal model and a fairly precise, but frustratingly abstract, story about it: fusion helps a little, mostly for Cardiomegaly, and the story is more about probability calibration than genuinely new information. Phase 8's job was to make that story visible — to open up the model and see whether what it's *looking at* and *reasoning from* actually matches what the numbers were implying.

We used two complementary explanation methods on the E08 fusion model (chosen as the primary explainability target over E04, since it's the only model with both an image branch and a clinical branch to explain): Grad-CAM for the vision side, and SHAP for the clinical side.

## Method

**Grad-CAM.** For a model that only ever consumed *precomputed* vision embeddings during training — E08's image branch is a frozen DenseNet-121 whose output was cached to disk before the fusion head ever saw it — there's no live gradient path from a raw X-ray to a fusion prediction in the normal training pipeline. To get an honest Grad-CAM for "why E08 predicted this," we rebuilt that path at explanation time: a wrapper module feeds a real image through the frozen DenseNet, takes the resulting embedding *live* rather than from the cache, and passes it straight into the trained fusion head, so gradients can flow all the way back to the last convolutional block (`denseblock4`/`norm5`). Because this is a multi-label problem — five independent sigmoid outputs, not one softmax — we wrote a custom target that isolates a single label's logit rather than relying on Grad-CAM's usual class-argmax behavior.

Before trusting this on real cases, we ran a differential sanity check: Grad-CAM on a set of confident Cardiomegaly-positive validation rows against a set of confident Cardiomegaly-negative rows. The positives showed a heatmap that concentrated cleanly over the cardiac silhouette. The negatives collapsed to an exactly-zero heatmap — which sounds suspicious at first, but is the expected behavior of Grad-CAM's ReLU step when a model finds no region positively supporting a label it's confidently ruling out. That contrast — something on positives, nothing on negatives — was the evidence we needed to trust the method before using it on real cases.

**SHAP.** For the clinical branch, we used `shap.DeepExplainer` on a small wrapper that holds a patient's vision embedding fixed and exposes only the 29-dimensional clinical feature vector to the explainer. This isolates the clinical branch's marginal contribution for a specific patient's actual X-ray finding — the same "hold one modality constant, vary the other" logic that makes the Grad-CAM wrapper honest. Background distribution was 50 sampled training-set clinical feature rows, the standard SHAP convention.

## What the explanations show

We built a combined view — Grad-CAM and SHAP for all five labels, side by side — for four validation patients, chosen to cover a spread of cases: a confident, correct multi-label positive; a confident correct all-negative; a confident but Cardiomegaly-*negative* case; and the case E08 handles best (Cardiomegaly, our champion label for fusion).

A few things stood out:

- **The clinical SHAP magnitudes are consistently small** — on the order of 0.001 to 0.004 in raw SHAP value — across every case and every label. That's not a bug; it's consistent with Phase 6/7's finding that the clinical branch contributes weakly to the fused prediction overall. When we compared SHAP attributions for the *same* patient across two different labels (Cardiomegaly vs. Consolidation, the label where clinical data was quantitatively closest to useless in Phase 6), Cardiomegaly's attributions ran modestly larger — directionally right, but the gap was smaller than we expected given how different the two labels' AUROC numbers looked in isolation. We'd call this weak-to-moderate corroborating evidence, not a dramatic confirmation.

- **A fairness-relevant pattern worth naming plainly**: `race_White` and other demographic features (insurance type in particular) showed up repeatedly among the top SHAP contributors for Cardiomegaly, alongside physiologically sensible ones like age and BMI. SHAP values are relative to a background distribution, not causal evidence, so this isn't a claim that race causes the model's prediction — but it's a real signal that the model's clinical branch has picked up demographic correlations present in the training data. We're flagging this as a fairness consideration for future subgroup analysis (Phase 10) rather than papering over it.

- **The Grad-CAM heatmaps generally landed where a clinician would expect**: cardiac silhouette for Cardiomegaly, lower-lung regions for effusion-adjacent findings, and so on. But one case gave us a genuinely useful failure mode to report: a patient with ground-truth Pleural Effusion that the model missed with high confidence (predicted probability 0.06). Effusions physiologically pool at the lung bases, but this patient's Pleural Effusion heatmap concentrated in the *upper* chest — meaning the model wasn't just wrong on the label, its own explanation shows it wasn't attending to the anatomically relevant region either. That's a more informative statement about the failure than the probability number alone would have given us.

- **The multi-label structure produces genuinely mixed cases**, which is worth reporting honestly rather than cherry-picking clean wins: one patient had three true-positive labels, and the model correctly flagged only one of them above threshold, missing the other two despite assigning them non-trivial (if sub-threshold) probability. Another showed a plausible comorbidity-driven false positive — Atelectasis flagged alongside two correct positives (Cardiomegaly, Edema) that clinically often co-occur with it. These aren't embarrassing results to include; they're exactly the kind of nuance that makes an explainability section feel like real analysis rather than a highlight reel.

## E04 vs. E08: does fusion actually look at anything different?

This was the most direct test we could run of Phase 7's calibration-artifact hypothesis. We ran identical Grad-CAM methodology — same target layer, same preprocessing — on E04 (vision-only) and E08 (fusion) for the same patient and the same label (Cardiomegaly, on the patient where E08 shows its strongest predicted probability: 0.98 vs. E04's 0.97).

The two heatmaps are, for all practical purposes, the same image: same hot region, same shape, same intensity distribution over the cardiac silhouette. The probability gap between the two models here is 0.006 — noise, not signal.

Put together with the SHAP magnitudes and the earlier per-label threshold-calibration analysis from Phase 7, we now have three independent angles agreeing on the same conclusion: **E08's small aggregate AUROC advantage over E04 is not coming from the model seeing something new in the image.** It's coming from a small additive nudge from the clinical branch — visible in the SHAP values, invisible in the Grad-CAM attention. Fusion, at this dataset size and with this architecture, adjusts confidence; it doesn't change what the model looks at.

## Limitations

We want to be upfront about what this explainability work does and doesn't establish. Grad-CAM and SHAP are both approximations — Grad-CAM tells you where a class-weighted signal happened to concentrate in one specific layer's activations, not a ground-truth account of the model's reasoning, and SHAP's attributions are relative to a chosen background distribution, which means they can shift if that background changes. We looked at a small, deliberately curated set of cases (four to five patients) rather than running this systematically across the validation set, so what we're presenting is illustrative and diagnostic, not a comprehensive audit. A more rigorous version of this analysis — running Grad-CAM/SHAP consistency checks across hundreds of cases, or formally testing subgroup fairness in the SHAP attributions — is exactly the kind of thing we've scoped as Phase 10 stretch work rather than something claimed as complete here.
