# Phase 10 — Robustness & Bias: Subgroup Fairness Testing

Phase 8's SHAP analysis flagged a correlational signal: `race_White` and `insurance_type` recurred among the top clinical-branch contributors for Cardiomegaly, which was reported as a fairness consideration rather than resolved. This sub-step follows up with a direct, quantitative test: does E04/E08's actual predictive *performance* — not just feature attribution — differ across demographic and clinical subgroups?

## Method

Four categorical subgroup columns are available in the processed manifest: `sex`, `race`, `ethnicity`, `insurance_type` (age and BMI are continuous and excluded from this analysis; categorical subgroup analysis is the appropriate lens for a discrete group-fairness question).

For each subgroup value, on the test split (n=1314), AUROC and ECE (10-bin, equal-width, matching the Phase 9 methodology) were computed per label, for both E04 (raw probabilities) and E08 (deployed/calibrated probabilities via `deployed_temperatures.json`, consistent with how E08 is evaluated everywhere else in this project). **Subgroups with fewer than 50 test examples were excluded** from metric computation — a deliberately conservative floor, chosen after Phase 9's equal-width ECE binning work showed that a single thin group can produce a misleadingly extreme number. 5 of the original subgroup values across the 4 columns were excluded on this basis (Pacific Islander n=23, Patient Refused n=4 in both `race` and `ethnicity`, Native American n=3, insurance "Other" n=37).

**What this measures, precisely:** for each (subgroup column, label, model), the max−min spread in AUROC and in ECE across its non-excluded subgroup values. A large spread means the model's ability to discriminate the label, or its calibration quality, varies substantially depending on which group the patient belongs to — the central question group-fairness analysis exists to answer, distinct from asking whether fusion (E08) outperforms vision-only (E04) within a given group, which was evaluated separately.

## Findings

**Race is the subgroup dimension with the largest and most consistent performance gaps, concentrated on Atelectasis.** On Atelectasis/E08, AUROC ranges from 0.604 (race: Other) to 0.740 (race: Black) — a spread of 0.136, the largest in the entire analysis. The same pattern holds for E04 (0.597 Other to 0.738 Asian, spread 0.142) — so this is not a fusion-specific artifact, it is present in the vision-only model too. All contributing groups have n≥50, so this spread is not a thin-sample effect.

**The same subgroup/label combination also shows the largest ECE spread**, and in a way that compounds rather than offsets the AUROC gap: Atelectasis/E08's ECE ranges from 0.151 (Black) to 0.292 (Unknown) across race groups. Separately, on Edema/E08, Black patients have both the *lowest* AUROC among race groups (0.682, vs. 0.734 for White) and one of the *highest* ECE values (0.217, second only to Unknown) — worse discrimination and worse calibration for the same subgroup on the same label, not two independent, unrelated gaps.

**Insurance type shows a real but smaller, and not uniformly directional, effect.** On Cardiomegaly/E08, Medicaid patients have the *highest* AUROC among insurance groups (0.821) while Medicare has the lowest (0.781) — the opposite direction from a naive assumption that under-resourced insurance categories would see worse performance. This is reported as observed, not interpreted causally; the direction is a finding to be honest about, not a pattern to force into an expected narrative.

**Sex shows a smaller, but consistent-across-both-models gap on Edema**: Female AUROC is lower than Male for both E04 (0.668 vs. 0.760) and E08 (0.662 vs. 0.755) — spread ≈0.093–0.094 in both cases. The consistency across independently-trained E04 and E08 architectures makes this a comparatively robust signal despite its smaller magnitude relative to the race/Atelectasis finding.

**Connecting back to Phase 8.** The SHAP analysis found `race_White` and `insurance_type` recurring among Cardiomegaly's top clinical-branch feature attributions, explicitly flagged as correlational rather than causal. This subgroup analysis adds a complementary, independent line of evidence: on Cardiomegaly specifically, race and insurance-type subgroup AUROC spreads are present but moderate (race spread ≈0.023–0.036 on Cardiomegaly, smaller than the Atelectasis finding); it is Atelectasis and Edema, not the label Phase 8 flagged, where the largest performance gaps by race actually appear. The two analyses agree that race-correlated signal exists in this system, but point to it mattering most on different labels than Phase 8's SHAP case studies happened to examine — a reminder that a feature-attribution finding on one label doesn't necessarily predict where the largest real performance gap will be.

## What this does and does not establish

This analysis demonstrates that measured performance (AUROC, ECE) varies across demographic subgroups by a magnitude too large, on groups too well-sampled (n≥50 throughout), to dismiss as noise. It does **not** establish why. Plausible contributing factors — none of which this analysis can distinguish between — include: imaging acquisition differences correlated with patient population in the source CheXpert Plus data (equipment, positioning, site-level practice variation), differing label prevalence or disease presentation by subgroup, sample composition and size differences across groups even above the n=50 floor, or genuine differential model behavior learned during training. Attributing the gap to any one of these without further investigation would be overclaiming.

## Limitations

- Four categorical columns were tested (sex, race, ethnicity, insurance_type); no interaction effects between columns were tested (e.g. race × sex jointly), which could reveal compounded disparities invisible to single-column analysis — out of scope here.
- The n=50 floor is conservative but arbitrary; a smaller floor would surface more (noisier) comparisons, a larger one fewer (more reliable) ones. The choice trades completeness for trustworthiness, consistent with the same tradeoff made in Phase 9's calibration binning work.
- ECE here uses 10 equal-width bins per subgroup, the same binning as the full-test-set calibration work — but subgroup sample sizes (minimum 50) are far smaller than the full test set (1314), so individual ECE bins within a subgroup computation can be thin even though the subgroup total meets the n=50 floor. This was not independently re-verified with quantile binning the way the full-dataset ECE anomaly was in Phase 9; subgroup ECE values should be read as indicative, not to the same precision standard as the full-dataset calibration numbers.
- This is a test-set snapshot, not a causal audit. No claim is made here about the mechanism producing these gaps, only that they exist and are large enough to warrant attention in any deployment discussion.
